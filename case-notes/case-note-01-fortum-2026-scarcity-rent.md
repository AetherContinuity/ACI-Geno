# Case Note 01 — Fortum, November 2026: Who Gets the Scarcity Rent?

**Working note, English.** Connects three documents: ACI SP-007 (theory), OGAS3 TP-001
(measurement), and a single corporate event (26.11.2026). Not fiction, not canon. Markings
follow the OGAS3 convention: **[L]** checked against a source, **[A]** assessment or
interpretation, **[M]** measured with a prior registration.

## 1. The three documents and their roles

| Document | Role | Core claim |
|---|---|---|
| [SP-007](https://github.com/AetherContinuity/aethercontinuity.github.io/blob/main/papers/sp-007-biological-constraints-institutional-continuity.html) | Theory | Decision window closure is neurocognitive before it is institutional. Status routing, conflict avoidance, success-prior lock-in, linear extrapolation, and narrative displacement systematically misroute early signals. |
| [TP-001](https://github.com/AetherContinuity/ogas3/blob/main/tyopaperit/tp-001-korjauskyky-ja-tulovirrat.md) | Measurement | Finland has correction capacity but no actor holding power, incentive, and capability at once; the paper measures income flows, leakage channels, and — in § 7 — the allocation of scarcity rents. |
| Fortum release 26.11.2026 [L] | Event | Business structure and leadership team renewed from 1.2.2027: three business units + three enabling functions; leadership team shrinks to seven; four of the incumbent members leave. |

The three use the same company as their specimen from different directions: SP-007 as a
theory of why its decisions misroute, TP-001 as a measurement of what the decisions cost
and who bears them, and the 26.11.2026 release as an observable change in the
decision-making geometry itself.

### 1.1 How the levels are proposed to connect — shown, not asserted

The linkage between TP-001 and SP-007 is a hypothesis of this note, [A] throughout, and
should be read as a proposed chain, not an established result:

1. **TP-001 (economic level):** the scarcity-rent question belongs to no one (CN-004)
   and no actor bears the cost at decision time (SM-007). This *describes* the actor
   deficit; it does not explain why the deficit persists instead of being closed by
   some actor with power, incentive, and capability.
2. **SP-007 (cognitive/social level):** supplies the persistence mechanism. Status
   routing means whoever raises the unowned question pays a status cost that the
   question's future value cannot repay to them personally; conflict avoidance keeps
   the silence stable because naming the allocation issue threatens every coalition
   member simultaneously (TP-001 § 7: "silence serves nearly everyone"); success-prior
   lock-in keeps the contractual path attractive after a validated success record.
3. **Testable consequence of the chain:** if the levels are genuinely linked, the
   *economic* structure (rent captured outside the domestic decision layer) should
   co-occur with the *process* signature SP-007 predicts (the question never formally
   raised by any domestic actor despite visible scarcity). Either half without the
   other supports only its own level — see prediction 4 in § 5.

## 3. Reading 1: TP-001 — the measurement

TP-001 § 6.1 and § 7 are about Fortum directly:

- **The Google PPA (signed 9.9.2026) [L]:** 22 years, 50 % of Loviisa capacity
  2030–2049. Loviisa produces ~10 % of Finnish electricity and needs ~1 bn € of
  investments by 2050 to continue. TP-001's interpretation [A]: the contract is a price
  risk transfer, not a capability shortage — a return-target-guided state-owned company,
  whose balance sheet carried the Russia and Uniper losses, sells baseload power to a
  foreign buyer. No direct evidence it would have acted differently without the losses.
- **Scarcity rent allocation under a PPA (§ 7) [A, mechanism]:** TP-001's table assigns
  the PPA rent to the buyer. The *generic* fixed-price PPA supports this: the buyer
  pays a fixed price and captures the difference when the market price rises above it,
  so the fixed price doubles as insurance against a price rise the buyer's own demand
  helps cause. But the allocation is **not** a property of the contract type alone. It
  depends on the pricing level, the settlement mechanism (financial difference vs
  physical delivery), the contracted volume share, any price floors or caps, and the
  buyer's net position outside the contract. The public record gives only headline
  terms (22 years, 50 % of Loviisa capacity 2030–2049) [L]; strike level, settlement
  form, and volume details are not public. Until the Fortum–Google contract structure
  is shown, the rent-to-buyer assignment is an interpretation of a contract type, not
  a finding about this contract.
- **Scarcity rent allocation under other instruments (§ 7) [L, via TP-001's sources]:**
  a two-way CfD (Poland) returns the surplus to the state or consumers; Mankala
  structures return it to owners. Guarantees of origin let the certificate buyer claim
  the environmental value, making Finnish consumers' residual mix fossiler than actual
  production (95 % of Finnish renewables are certified; GoO prices 1–2 €/MWh).
- **The corrected core [L]:** the paper's original draft claimed the scarcity cost was
  still in the future. It was not. Nordic system price: 39.7 €/MWh (2025) → 82.4 €/MWh
  (2026, realised and forecast, LSEG 5/2026); hydrological deficit −24.3 TWh in
  March 2026. The PPA was signed *when scarcity was already visible*. The mechanism is
  therefore not that the cost lies ahead, but that **no actor bears it at the moment of
  signing** (SM-007) and **the question belongs to no one** (CN-004).
- **Why the question is never asked [A]:** silence serves nearly everyone — government
  wants investment, municipalities want property tax, producers want certain revenue,
  data centers want electricity and insurance.

## 4. Reading 2: SP-007 — the mechanism

SP-007 explains why TP-001's § 7 silence is stable rather than accidental:

- **Status routing.** Information flows through status positions, not epistemic quality.
  The 26.11.2026 reorganisation *reduces the number of formal status positions* through
  which domain-specific early warnings (nuclear asset integrity, renewables project
  slippage, grid constraints) can reach the top table: nuclear, renewables, and
  sustainability each lose a dedicated seat, and all fold into one Generation portfolio
  [L, the seats]. **That routing actually degrades is the hypothesis, not the finding
  [A].** Removing a formal position does not by itself remove informal influence:
  departing incumbents retain networks and expertise, escalation practices may
  substitute for seats, and whether one Generation EVP can attend to four domains at
  once is an empirical question about attention, not structure. Public organisational
  data cannot settle it — which is what the process observations in § 5 are for.
- **Conflict avoidance.** "Simpler governance" is coalition-maintenance language: it is
  true, and it is also the form that consensus pressure takes when stated aloud [A].
- **Success-prior lock-in.** The one internal promotion to a consolidated portfolio is
  a hydro executive with 35 years' tenure. Fortum's recent record (post-Uniper refocus,
  divestments, 99 % CO₂-free generation, 924 M€ comparable operating profit 2025) is a
  validated success prior. The reorganisation is coherent with that prior; whether the
  prior still matches the environment is exactly the question SP-007 says the
  architecture cannot process from the inside [A].
- **External substrate preserved [L].** Segment reporting stays unchanged and the
  strategy is pinned to dated public KPIs (2.2.2027). Whatever happens inside the
  seven-person coalition, external reference points persist and can be audited against
  it. This is SP-007 § 05's design principle — instruments as situational-awareness
  substrates outside the social layer — applied, likely unintentionally.

## 5. What would distinguish the readings

TP-001's own falsifiability discipline applies here. The strongest alternative
explanation is mundane and good: the company shrank through divestments, and a smaller
company needs a smaller leadership team (Inderes: "logical" [L]). There is **no failure
in this case**. The SP-007 reading is therefore not that Fortum is failing — it is that
the reorganisation is an observable instance of the *mechanism* (status-position
consolidation, prior-coherent promotion) operating in a non-failure mode, which is
rarer and more instructive: the information-routing geometry changes *before* anyone
knows whether it mattered [A].

**Symmetry requirement.** Outcome observations alone are weak evidence in both
directions, and the criteria must be stated so neither reading can win by default:

- Late or externally surfaced problems do **not** by themselves prove the SP-007
  mechanism — bad luck, sector-wide conditions, or ordinary post-reorganisation
  friction produce the same outcome signature.
- The *absence* of problems does **not** disprove the mechanism — it may simply never
  have been tested within the observation window.
- KPIs being met says nothing about routing quality: a coalition can hit its targets
  while systematically misrouting signals whose costs fall due later. That is SM-007's
  core claim, and it applies to the right-sizing reading's evidence too.

Therefore the distinguishing evidence must include **process observations, not only
outcomes** [A]:

- Where domain-specific findings originate: internal escalation (minutes, internal
  audit, leadership-team papers) vs regulators, media, or neighbours arriving first.
- Whether sustainability, renewables, and nuclear issues still appear as standing
  items in leadership-team materials after 1.2.2027, or enter only reactively.
- Expert retention in the folded domains: whether domain expertise follows the
  removed seats out of the decision perimeter.
- Attention allocation of the single Generation EVP across four domains (agenda
  structure, delegation patterns).

Distinguishing predictions (process + outcome):

1. **SP-007 reading supported if:** domain-specific issues (nuclear licensing,
   renewables slippage, grid limits) surface late or externally **and** the process
   data show the corresponding signals were absent from internal escalation channels
   before surfacing [A].
2. **Right-sizing reading supported if:** outcomes are met **and** process data show
   domain signals still routing normally — standing items present, findings of
   internal origin, stable expert retention [A].
3. **TP-001 reading supported if:** the scarcity-rent question remains unasked in
   Finnish public debate through the 2.2.2027 strategy disclosure, while the physical
   forcing point approaches — TP-001 § 8 notes Ireland's correction came from a *grid*
   limit, not a political decision, and identifies Finland's equivalent as the
   **connection queue (liittymäjono)** [L].
4. **Linkage (§ 1.1) supported if:** predictions 1 and 3 hold *together* — the
   economic structure (rent captured outside the domestic decision layer) and the
   process signature (the question never formally raised despite visible scarcity)
   co-occur. Either half alone supports only its own level, not the connection [A].

## 6. H3 — Firm Capacity Allocation Concentration

**Status: hypothesis, [A] throughout, registered for tracking.** Not measured; not yet
supported or refuted. Registered now, before the 2.2.2027 strategy disclosure, so that
the explanation does not form after the measurement (TP-001 § 10.2's discipline).

**Statement.** Fortum increases, over the long term, the share of its dispatchable and
low-emission production capacity allocated to large corporate customers under
long-term contracts, so that the commercially free capacity available to the domestic
electricity system shrinks.

### 6.1 The distinction that makes H3 worth registering

The scenario does not require selling power plants. A subtler and
owner-politically easier change is the reallocation of the *economic rights* to
production and of long-term capacity to large corporate customers. The Google PPA
already exhibits the form [L]: up to 50 % of Loviisa output in 2030–2049; the contract
supports Fortum's roughly 1 bn € Loviisa life-extension programme; and no equivalent
new production is added — what is rearranged is the commercial benefit and the price
risk of existing capacity. The plant can stay in Finland, in Fortum's ownership, and
on the grid, while its economic yield is committed elsewhere for two decades.

### 6.2 Relevance of the 26.11.2026 change

The new structure creates a dedicated Corporate Customers and Markets responsibility
in the leadership team, and the strategy emphasises responding to customer needs,
third-party-sourced energy, and partnerships to unlock new demand [L]. This does not
prove allocation transfer to large customers; it shows the position of corporate
customers strengthening in the business model. One distinction matters: the 26.11.
announcement concerns the leadership team and business structure, **not the Board of
Directors** — and the Board is the body that sets the direction of strategy [L]. H3's
test therefore lies ahead of the governance change, not behind it.

### 6.3 Development paths

| Scenario | What happens |
|---|---|
| A. Current | Long-term PPAs complement the spot market. |
| B. Concentrating | A growing share of nuclear and hydro output is bound to large customers' long-term contracts. |
| C. Structural | Fortum increasingly becomes the energy partner of heavy industry and data centers, with the commercial use of its capacity optimised to these customers' needs. |

H3 tracks paths B and C. Hydro deserves particular attention: it is not only produced
electricity but balancing capability needed at the whole Nordic system level. If its
commercial use is bound long-term to specific customer aggregates, the effect can
extend far beyond a single electricity contract [A].

### 6.4 Measurement variables

Measurable in principle; none yet measured:

- Share of nuclear output under long-term PPAs.
- Long-term contract and customer concentration of hydro.
- Changes in the commercial allocation of balancing capability.
- Large customers' share of Fortum's contract portfolio.
- Development of production tied outside the domestic spot market.

### 6.5 Prior and relations

**Prior [L].** Fortum's own history documents earlier divestments of businesses and
assets, including small hydro and electricity distribution. This does not evidence the
present development, but it makes the reorganisation of assets and businesses a
familiar operating mode for the company.

**Relation to TP-001 § 7 [A].** TP-001 asks who receives the *scarcity rent* when
prices rise; H3 asks who receives the *capacity itself* over time. These are the same
question at different horizons, and both share the structural feature that the answer
is settled by contract rather than by any domestic decision-making process.

**The larger question [A].** If the Finnish state holds a majority of Fortum and the
company allocates a growing share of strategically important production capacity to
international large customers, at what point do owner steering and the national
electricity-system interest diverge? Owner steering sits with VNK, system operation
with Fingrid, regulation with Energiavirasto — the question again belongs to no single
actor (CN-004), and no actor bears the divergence cost at decision time (SM-007). This
is a much larger question than one company's strategy change, and it is the point at
which the Fortum case would stop being a case study and become a test of the corpus's
central claim.
