## Exhibit B

**National Banana Security Index (BKHI), methodological note (extract)**

The index measures the State's ability to keep bananas available to its own citizens under all conditions. Production capacity alone does not guarantee security of supply. What matters is how much of production actually remains for citizens after export commitments, energy costs and other business obligations.

> **Availability base** = production − exports − waste − other committed use.

> **Availability gap (BSV)** = domestic demand − quantity actually available for domestic use.
> A positive BSV indicates a security-of-supply deficit.

**Strategic principle.** Production capacity is not the same thing as security of supply.

*Index value, most recent year: 0.002 bananas per resident. The Ministry of Finance describes this as "the first slice."*

## Exhibit C

**Environmental Impact Assessment: risk of primate escape (extract)**
*Issued by the regional environment authority*

A population of 120 green monkeys (*Chlorocebus sabaeus*) and capuchins (*Cebus capucinus*) is to be introduced to the dome to provide natural pollination and canopy management.

**Finding.** The risk of escape is moderate, particularly from April to October. Mustakorpi lies at the end of an ecological corridor leading to the Puijo nature reserve. Escaped individuals are expected to reach the old spruce stands of Puijo within four to six days.

**Winter survival.** Low risk. The population will not survive temperatures below −15 °C and will return to the data centre's condensate pipes.

**Worst-case scenario ("the Siege of Puijo").** If escaped primates learn to make systematic foraging trips to the greenhouse loading docks, the losses will be classified under *other committed use*.

**Mitigation.** Signs are to be placed on all Puijo trails:

*DO NOT FEED THE MONKEY – IT EATS DOMESTIC SECURITY OF SUPPLY.*

**Conclusion.** The project is environmentally feasible, provided that the primate population remains committed to domestic banana production and does not seek export markets on its own initiative via Puijo.

## Exhibit D

**Constitutional Court, ruling of 3.3.2027 (extract)**
*On the anticipatory right to use a banana (EBK)*

Section 2 of the Constitution provides that no future banana shall be eaten. The section prohibits an act (eating), not a property (the banana).

A banana that does not yet exist is not in law a banana. It is an *expectation*. An expectation may be owned, pledged, valued and transferred. It cannot be eaten, because eating requires a physical object.

An expectation may not give rise to an obligation to eat a banana that does not exist.

**On the first bunch to ripen.** The first bunch to ripen is owned by the State. Its right of use belongs to the pledgee. Its right of enjoyment belongs to the Puijo Capuchin Association. Its book value belongs to BANA, which has already entered it on its balance sheet. And it may be eaten by whoever gets there first, provided that it is not a future banana.

*No future banana shall be eaten. No future banana shall be budgeted. No future banana shall be presumed to exist without a forecast audit.*

## 7.11.2034, 18:14

Loading Dock 4 is the warmest outdoor place at Mustakorpi, because the data centre's exhaust duct runs beneath it. The capuchins are sitting on it, as they have sat every morning for a year and a half, and this evening they have not left. The Dock Book lies open between them at a page where the evening column is empty. A man in a frost-maintenance jacket stands at the end of the dock with a thermal probe in his hand, as if he had come to measure something and forgotten what.

Ten metres away, beside the airlock of Greenhouse 3, a small display shows two lines: the time and the date. Behind the glass, at the height of a man's chest, hangs a yellow bunch.

At 18:14 the display is unambiguous.

At 18:15 the date line goes blank.

No error message appears. The system simply leaves the field empty, because no value has been given for it. It does not know which day it is showing, so it shows nothing. The time keeps running.

A hand rises toward the display, as if to touch it, and stops in the air.

No one says anything.
