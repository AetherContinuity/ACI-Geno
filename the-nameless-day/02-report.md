# Part Two: The Report

**BANA – Agency for Banana Economy and Investment Assessment**
Inspection Report 1/IX
Subject: Greenhouse 3, Row 14 – loss of Bunch 1/2033
Recorded by: K.

*Note: Pages 41–44 (final statement) are missing. The table of contents has been approved as a formality.*

---

## Entry

The entry that follows the empty evening column in the Dock Book is dated the way the Association dates everything, by the day it actually was:

| Date | Trucks | Bananas | Remarks |
|---|--:|--:|---|
| 8.11.2034 | 0 | 142 | Bunch. 0 edible. |

By the state calendar it was the first day of the Month of Promise, Year IX, an Initiative Day. The Month of Promise carries the official meaning *everything is possible*. That morning, it no longer held.

The bunch was found in Greenhouse 3, Row 14, at 6.14 a.m., by the same member of the Association who had seen it flower. He did not touch it. He walked back to the loading dock and wrote it down.

The bunch hung from its stalk, brown and soft. The skins were whole. There were no bite marks. No one had eaten any of it. BANA classified the cause of loss as *natural*. In the Bananna State this was unheard of.

The Association reported the death to BANA, because BANA's number was the only one printed in the Association's rules. BANA informed the Ministry of Finance. The Ministry informed the insurer. The insurer informed the pledgee. The pledgee was the Kingdom of Sweden, and the Kingdom of Sweden sent a customs inspector.

---

Customs Inspector A. Strömberg of the Haparanda customs office arrived by train the next day, the second day of the Month of Promise, a Preparation Day. He carried a briefcase, a thermos, and a copy of the Dock Book, which Swedish Customs had bought three years earlier for three kilos of lingonberry jam.

He took a room at the Puijo lodge, an old timber house on the wooded hill above Kuopio, with the observation tower on one side and the glass dome on the other. Two guests had arrived on the same train: V. and the undersigned. The Ministry of the Future had commissioned us to assess the harvesting protocol for the bunch and had not cancelled the commission. The harvesting protocol was no longer needed.

Strömberg shook our hands and addressed me as Director-General. I corrected him. He did not record the correction.

He asked for every document concerning the bunch. The next morning he received 1,200 pages in fourteen binders. He read them in two days at the kitchen table, with the thermos at his elbow. He was the first person to read all of them. No one had expected this, and no one had prepared for it.

Binder 9 contained a printout from the access control system of Greenhouse 3, Airlock 3. Strömberg turned its pages quickly, because they were lists of times and names. On the last page was a line with a time and no date:

| Date | Time | Event |
|---|---|---|
| | 18:15:00 | DATE INVALID. ACCESS PENDING. |

I recorded the binder as received. I did not record the line, because it was a line in a document already received.

---

On the evening of the second day Strömberg laid two papers side by side on the table. One was a page from the Dock Book. The other was BANA's flowering notice, with the archive index stamp in its upper corner.

"According to the Dock Book, the flowering was observed on the twenty-second of April 2033," he said. "According to BANA, on the seventeenth of June 2033."

"Yes," I said.

"Two observations."

He wrote it in his notebook: *Two observations.* He looked at the two dates for a while. Then he put the Dock Book page aside, because it was not an official document, and took a third paper, the ripening forecast, which gave the second of January 2035.

"The bunch died fifty-five days before it would have been ripe," he said. "A banana does not rot before it ripens."

He looked at me, and then at V.

"Someone did this."

V. said nothing. I recorded it.

---

On the third morning Strömberg wanted to see Row 14. The airlock display showed the time and the date: the third day of the Month of Promise, Coordination Day. The door opened for him without delay. He did not look at the display, because there was nothing on it to look at.

On the way back he stopped at the north gate of the dome.

On the ground beside the glass wall lay a pair of sunglasses, a scarf and a carrot. Beneath them was a frozen puddle. It reached all the way to the glass, and hardly at all in the other direction.

"A local custom?" he asked.

"A snowman," I said.

"Where is he?"

"He was here."

Strömberg looked at the puddle for a moment and walked on. He wrote nothing in his notebook. I recorded the puddle, because this is a BANA inspection report, and BANA records everything.

---

That evening Strömberg asked whether the statement Sweden had sent as pledgee had arrived. It had been posted on the twenty-seventh of October. No one knew. He checked BANA's registry. Against the statement it said: *Sent by Bananna State Post, 25 B commemorative stamp. Received: no.*

"Snail mail," he said, to no one.

---

At dusk he walked out to Loading Dock 4. The capuchins were sitting there, as they had sat every morning since the flowering. It was evening now, and they had not left.

"Who did this?" Strömberg asked.

The Association's eldest member answered. He had escaped from the dome in its first summer, as the environmental impact assessment had said someone would.

"We counted."

"What did you count?"

"Whose it was."

"And whose was it?"

"The eater's."

Strömberg waited. No one went on.

"Who ate it?"

"No one," said the eldest. "That is why you are here."

---

That night Strömberg wrote the first line in his notebook that was not a quotation from a document:

*Sabotage. Motive: ?*

He was wrong. I know that now. I did not know it then, and I recorded it as he said it.

---

## Association Hearing

*Hearing record 1/IX*

**Time:** Month of Promise 4, Stakeholder Day (11.11.2034)
**Place:** Loading Dock 4
**Present:** Customs Inspector A. Strömberg (examining); T. Kapusiini, chair of the Puijo Capuchin Association (no relation); the Association's eldest member; V.; the undersigned (record)

Strömberg wanted to hold the hearing at the lodge. The Association declined. The hearing was held on the dock, because the witnesses would not leave sight of the scene.

---

**Strömberg:** Where were you between the twenty-seventh of October and the seventh of November?

**Chair:** Here.

**Strömberg:** The whole time?

**Chair:** Mornings here. Nights in the pipe.

**Strömberg:** What were you doing here?

**Chair:** Counting.

**Strömberg:** Counting what?

**Chair:** In the morning, how many bananas there are in the bunch. In the evening, how many are left.

Strömberg asked for the accounts. The chair tore a page from the Dock Book and handed it over. In accordance with BANA's document instructions, I added a column for the state calendar:

| Date | State calendar (added by K.) | Bananas | Remarks |
|---|---|--:|---|
| 27.10.2034 | Month of Reporting 25 | 142 | Yellow. The snowman is weeping. |
| 28.10.–5.11. | Month of Reporting 26–30; Additional Days 1–4 | 142 | (as in Exhibit H) |
| 6.11. | Additional Day 5 | 142 | Soft. Of the snowman, the glasses and the carrot remain. |
| 7.11. | ? | 142 | Counted. Belongs to the eater. |
| 8.11. | Month of Promise 1, IX | 142 | Bunch. 0 edible. |

*Recorder's note:* The conversion table under Decree 1/I contains five named additional days. The entry for 7.11. has no equivalent. The entry is probably an error by the Association or a later addition.

---

**Strömberg:** There are 142 bananas every day.

**Chair:** No one ate.

**Strömberg:** Did the bunch ripen?

**Chair:** Yes.

**Strömberg:** When?

**Chair:** When the snowman began to weep.

**Strömberg:** I asked for a date.

**Chair:** It is in the book.

Strömberg looked at the page and then at his notebook.

**Strömberg:** According to BANA's forecast, the bunch would have ripened in January.

**Chair:** BANA does not look at the snowman.

*Recorder's note:* The Association's language is at times descriptive. The entries in the Remarks column are observations of the bunch's appearance and of the weather. They are not relevant to the investigation.

---

**Strömberg:** If the bunch was ripe and belonged to the eater, why did you not eat it?

**Eldest member:** We had not finished counting.

**Strömberg:** When did you finish?

**Eldest member:** In the evening. On the day that has no name.

*Recorder's note:* See above. The day in question does not exist.

**Strömberg:** Who benefits from the death of the bunch?

**Chair:** Everyone who has a paper on it.

Strömberg wrote in his notebook: *Paper = insurance?* He underlined *insurance* twice.

**Strömberg:** Thank you. That will do for now.

**Chair:** We are here.

---

*Recorder's summary:* On the evidence of the hearing, the Association had neither motive nor opportunity. Its accounts show that the bunch was not touched. One entry is incorrectly dated. The entries in the Remarks column are irrelevant. The investigation will proceed in the direction of the insurer.

Signed, K.

---

In the evening V. read the record. He underlined one word in it. I did not record which.

## The Night Dock

*Recorder's note, the night between the fourth and fifth days of the Month of Promise (11–12.11.2034)*

Strömberg was reading the insurance contract at the kitchen table. It was eleven o'clock. He had asked the insurer's local representative for the contract that evening, and the representative had delivered it promptly. No one had ever before asked him for a contract in order to read it.

The contract ran to nine pages. On the eighth, Strömberg stopped and read aloud:

> Should the work in progress fail to be completed before the forecast completion date (2.1.2035), the insurer shall compensate the assessed value of the work, 2,400,000,000 Banana Units.

He put the paper down.

"A living bunch is worth one bunch," he said. "A dead bunch is worth the national debt."

He was not speaking to me. He underlined the name of the beneficiary. I did not see the name, because he turned the page.

I recorded: *Strömberg has found a motive.*

---

At half past eleven I went outside. I could not sleep. V. followed, as he does when there is no reason to stay in.

We walked to Loading Dock 4. It was empty. The capuchins were in the condensate pipe, because it was night and twelve degrees below zero. For the first time in a year and a half, no one was sitting on the dock.

We sat on the edge. The boards were cold. The data centre's exhaust duct ran beneath the dock, and its warmth reached our ankles but not the wood.

I have built a dock. I know that a dock is finished only when someone can step onto it. This dock had been finished since 2026. In a year and a half it had been stepped on by one driver, 412 trucks and the Association. Of bananas, one peel had passed over it.

V. looked at the dome. I looked in the same direction.

---

At 0.20 a vehicle approached from the direction of Puijo. It was a Bananna State Post delivery van. It drove slowly, because in the Bananna State postal vans always drive slowly, and stopped at the end of the dock.

The driver got out with a letter in his hand and read the address under the lamp.

"Greenhouse three, row fourteen," he read.

"The row is inside the dome," I said.

"Can post go in there?"

"No."

He handed the letter to me, because I was nearest, and asked for a signature. I signed. On the envelope was a 25 B commemorative stamp: a laurel-crowned man holding a banana toward the sun. The postmark was 27.10.2034. The sender was Swedish Customs, on behalf of the pledgee.

The letter had been in transit for sixteen days. From Haparanda to Kuopio is 420 kilometres. The letter had travelled 26 kilometres a day.

The driver drove away slowly.

I recorded: *Received: statement of the Swedish pledgee, addressed to the bunch. Addressee deceased.*

---

Before we went back, we walked to the north gate. The sunglasses and the scarf lay where they had lain, on top of the puddle, and the puddle had frozen hard. The carrot was gone.

I looked around in the lamplight. In the snow there were small tracks leading toward the condensate pipe. They were four-footed, and beside them ran a thin line, as if a tail had been dragged.

I recorded: *Evidence removed from the scene.*

V. bent down and pressed his palm against the glass of the dome, where the puddle met it. He held it there for a while. Then he looked at me.

I pressed my own palm against the glass beside his. The glass was warm, on the outside, at twelve degrees below zero.

I recorded: *Glass warm. Dome heating operating as planned.*

---

At 1.05 we returned to the lodge. Strömberg was still at the kitchen table with the contract. I gave him the letter.

He opened it and read it. It was one page. He read it twice, folded it back into the envelope and put the envelope in his breast pocket.

"Routine," he said. "The pledgee's statement. It no longer matters."

"What does it say?"

"That the pledgee does not object."

"To what?"

He looked at me as if the question were unnecessary.

"To the harvest."

Then he went back to page eight.

I recorded: *Content of statement: routine (Strömberg).* He did not ask me to enter the statement in the registry, and I did not.

---

I recorded the events of the night in order:

1. Strömberg found a motive.
2. The Swedish statement arrived sixteen days late.
3. The carrot disappeared.
4. The glass of the dome was warm.

None of them concerned the insurance.

It was 1.40 when I went to bed. By the state calendar, the fifth day of the Month of Promise had begun. It was Consultation Day.

---

## In Three Calendars

*Recorder's note, the fifth and sixth days of the Month of Promise (12–13.11.2034)*

Strömberg spread three sheets of paper across the kitchen table and taped them together. With a ruler he drew a timeline from the flowering to the death of the bunch. At the flowering he wrote BANA's date, 17.6.2033, and at the end 8.11.2034. The forecast ripening date, 2.1.2035, he marked in red off the end of the timeline, on the edge of the table, where there was no room for it.

"The bunch never got this far," he said, and tapped the red mark.

Then he asked for the minutes of every body that had dealt with the bunch between the twenty-seventh of October and the seventh of November. He received eleven binders that afternoon. Binder 11 also contained a letter he had not asked for: the seventh polite inquiry from the harbour master at Narvik, asking when the first banana shipment of the Bananna State's Silk Road might be expected. It had been filed under *Silk Road, pending*. Strömberg set it aside.

I recorded: *Binder 11 contains an irrelevant document.*

---

Strömberg went through the meetings day by day and marked each on the timeline. In the state calendar every day has a name, and on every named day a meeting had been held. On Consultation Day a statement had been requested. On Steering Group Day the steering group had met. On Interim Report Day an interim report had been prepared. On Impact Day BANA had assessed impact. On Follow-on Funding Day follow-on funding had been sought.

On the additional days there had been festivities, and every festivity had minutes.

"Everyone has an alibi," Strömberg said. "Every body, for every day. Everyone was in a meeting."

He leaned back.

"So the culprit needed no hands. The weapon is the contract."

He showed me page eight of the insurance contract. The name of the beneficiary was underlined. Now I saw it: *Ministry of Finance.*

"If the bunch dies before the second of January, the Ministry receives the national debt. No one has to do anything. It is enough that no one does anything. The contract does the rest."

I recorded: *The calendars support Strömberg's theory.*

V. looked at the timeline for a long time. His gaze stopped at the point where the additional days ended and Year IX began. On the timeline the gap was one day wide. Strömberg had left it empty.

---

On the morning of the sixth day of the Month of Promise, Steering Group Day, Strömberg wanted to hear the observer from the Food and Agriculture Organization. He had found the name in binder 4, on the last line of a distribution list: *Paredes, E. (FAO), for information.*

Don Esteban Paredes lived in the service building of Greenhouse 3, because it was warm there. He had been in Kuopio since 2031. He came from Ecuador, from the province of El Oro, and he had grown bananas for forty years. In his room there was a bed, a kettle and a wall calendar that was neither of the Bananna State's calendars. It contained only numbers: days since flowering.

He spoke English slowly and precisely.

"I am here since 2031," he said. "Nobody asks me. You are the first."

"What is your task?" Strömberg asked.

"To observe. I observe. I write reports. Nobody reads."

"Did you see the bunch?"

"Every day."

"When did it ripen?"

Paredes looked at his wall calendar. When he answered, he did not speak English. He spoke the way a man speaks when he is counting in his head in his own language.

"La flor salió el veintidós de abril. El racimo estaba listo el veintisiete. Doce días para comerlo. Nadie vino."

Strömberg waited for a translation. Paredes did not translate, because he considered the matter clear.

I recorded: *Witness answered in Spanish. Translation missing. BANA has no Spanish translator. Translation commissioned from the Ministry of the Future.*

"Do you understand?" Strömberg asked me.

"No."

He turned to V. V. was looking at Paredes. He said nothing.

---

Before we left, Strömberg asked one more question, perhaps out of courtesy.

"What do you think of the Bananna State?"

Paredes smiled for the first time.

"O. Henry wrote 'banana republic' about Honduras. Nineteen-oh-four. A real banana republic has bananas. It has too many bananas, and the wrong people eat them."

He looked out of the window at Greenhouse 3.

"Here, nobody eats. This is something else. I don't have the word."

I recorded: *Witness does not have the word.*

---

In the afternoon Strömberg added a fourth sheet to the timeline. On it he set out three calendars side by side, as I had done on the page of the Dock Book:

| | State calendar | Association calendar | Paredes's calendar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flowering | 17.6. VII (BANA conversion 17.6.2033) | the day of the flower | *el veintidós de abril* |
| Ripening | forecast 2.1.2035 | "when the snowman began to weep" | *el veintisiete* |
| Edible | – | – | *doce días* |
| Death | Month of Promise 1, IX | 8.11. 0 edible. | *nadie vino* |

*Recorder's note:* Of the three calendars only one is official. The other two are recorded for completeness.

Strömberg looked at the table and nodded.

"Three calendars, one truth," he said. "The bunch died before it ripened. The contract pays. The Ministry benefits."

That evening he called Haparanda and asked for authority to hear the Ministry of Finance formally. Authority was granted.

After the call he took the pledgee's statement from his breast pocket, looked at the envelope and put it back unopened.

V. went to bed early. Before he did, he stopped at the table and touched the empty place on the timeline with his forefinger, the day between Year VIII and Year IX that had no name.

I recorded: *V. examined the timeline.*
