# Part Two: The Report

**BANA – Agency for Banana Economy and Investment Assessment**
Inspection Report 1/IX
Subject: Greenhouse 3, Row 14 – loss of Bunch 1/2033
Recorded by: K.

*Note: Pages 41–44 (final statement) are missing. The table of contents has been approved as a formality.*

---

## Entry

The entry that follows the empty evening column in the Dock Book is dated the way the Association dates everything, by the day it actually was:

| Date | Trucks | Bananas | Remarks |
|---|--:|--:|---|
| 8.11.2034 | 0 | 0 | Bunch. 0 edible. |

By the state calendar it was the first day of the Month of Promise, Year IX, an Initiative Day. The Month of Promise carries the official meaning *everything is possible*. That morning, it no longer held.

The bunch was found in Greenhouse 3, Row 14, at 6.14 a.m., by the same member of the Association who had seen it flower. He did not touch it. He walked back to the loading dock and wrote it down.

The bunch hung from its stalk, brown and soft. The skins were whole. There were no bite marks. No one had eaten any of it. BANA classified the cause of loss as *natural*. In the Bananna State this was unheard of.

The Association reported the death to BANA, because BANA's number was the only one printed in the Association's rules. BANA informed the Ministry of Finance. The Ministry informed the insurer. The insurer informed the pledgee. The pledgee was the Kingdom of Sweden, and the Kingdom of Sweden sent a customs inspector.

---

Customs Inspector A. Strömberg of the Haparanda customs office arrived by train the next day, the second day of the Month of Promise, a Preparation Day. He carried a briefcase, a thermos, and a copy of the Dock Book, which Swedish Customs had bought three years earlier for three kilos of lingonberry jam.

He took a room at the Puijo lodge, an old timber house on the wooded hill above Kuopio, with the observation tower on one side and the glass dome on the other. Two guests had arrived on the same train: V. and the undersigned. The Ministry of the Future had commissioned us to assess the harvesting protocol for the bunch and had not cancelled the commission. The harvesting protocol was no longer needed.

Strömberg shook our hands and addressed me as Director-General. I corrected him. He did not record the correction.

He asked for every document concerning the bunch. The next morning he received 1,200 pages in fourteen binders. He read them in two days at the kitchen table, with the thermos at his elbow. He was the first person to read all of them. No one had expected this, and no one had prepared for it.

Binder 9 contained a printout from the access control system of Greenhouse 3, Airlock 3. Strömberg turned its pages quickly, because they were lists of times and names. On the last page was a line with a time and no date:

| Date | Time | Event |
|---|---|---|
| | 18:15:00 | DATE INVALID. ACCESS PENDING. |

I recorded the binder as received. I did not record the line, because it was a line in a document already received.

---

On the evening of the second day Strömberg laid two papers side by side on the table. One was a page from the Dock Book. The other was BANA's flowering notice, with the archive index stamp in its upper corner.

"According to the Dock Book, the flowering was observed on the twenty-second of April 2033," he said. "According to BANA, on the seventeenth of June 2033."

"Yes," I said.

"Two observations."

He wrote it in his notebook: *Two observations.* He looked at the two dates for a while. Then he put the Dock Book page aside, because it was not an official document, and took a third paper, the ripening forecast, which gave the second of January 2035.

"The bunch died fifty-five days before it would have been ripe," he said. "A banana does not rot before it ripens."

He looked at me, and then at V.

"Someone did this."

V. said nothing. I recorded it.

---

On the third morning Strömberg wanted to see Row 14. The airlock display showed the time and the date: the third day of the Month of Promise, Coordination Day. The door opened for him without delay. He did not look at the display, because there was nothing on it to look at.

On the way back he stopped at the north gate of the dome.

On the ground beside the glass wall lay a pair of sunglasses, a scarf and a carrot. Beneath them was a frozen puddle. It reached all the way to the glass, and hardly at all in the other direction.

"A local custom?" he asked.

"A snowman," I said.

"Where is he?"

"He was here."

Strömberg looked at the puddle for a moment and walked on. He wrote nothing in his notebook. I recorded the puddle, because this is a BANA inspection report, and BANA records everything.

---

That evening Strömberg asked whether the statement Sweden had sent as pledgee had arrived. It had been posted on the twenty-seventh of October. No one knew. He checked BANA's registry. Against the statement it said: *Sent by Bananna State Post, 25 B commemorative stamp. Received: no.*

"Snail mail," he said, to no one.

---

At dusk he walked out to Loading Dock 4. The capuchins were sitting there, as they had sat every morning since the flowering. It was evening now, and they had not left.

"Who did this?" Strömberg asked.

The Association's eldest member answered. He had escaped from the dome in its first summer, as the environmental impact assessment had said someone would.

"We counted."

"What did you count?"

"Whose it was."

"And whose was it?"

"The eater's."

Strömberg waited. No one went on.

"Who ate it?"

"No one," said the eldest. "That is why you are here."

---

That night Strömberg wrote the first line in his notebook that was not a quotation from a document:

*Sabotage. Motive: ?*

He was wrong. I know that now. I did not know it then, and I recorded it as he said it.
